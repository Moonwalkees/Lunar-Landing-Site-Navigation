# 6.1 Lunar eclipse (Earth occults the Sun)

Status: open
Owner:
Source: scope.md > Model (Power, Battery), Core 4 Metrics (Longest night, survives)
Priority: high
Depends on: 1.2, 3.1

## Description
During a lunar eclipse the Earth blocks the Sun as seen from the lunar surface, for up to ~3.5 h. Compute the Earth-Sun angular separation and the Earth and Sun angular radii from the ephemeris (1.2), then the overlap fraction of the Sun disc covered by the Earth disc (circle-circle intersection area). Multiply the terrain disc fraction `f` from 3.1 by `(1 - occulted_fraction)`. Include the Earth's apparent radius (~0.95° from the Moon).

## Why it is useful
Eclipse hours are an extra, unplanned "night" on top of the lunar night. Mission designers explicitly size batteries and thermal survival for them.

## Impact of not having it
The model reports full power during an eclipse. A site can show "survives: yes" when a ~3 h eclipse just before sunset would drain the battery. It also overstates sunlit % and Wh/day for windows containing eclipses. Core numbers are optimistic and wrong in exactly the scenario judges may test.

## Expected user interaction
A window containing a lunar eclipse shows a power dip to 0 W for a few hours on the time series, and the longest night and battery minimum reflect it.

## Done when
- Overlap fraction is 0 when separated, 1 when fully covered, and matches a known circle-intersection value in between (unit test)
- A known eclipse date (e.g. a documented total lunar eclipse) produces a power dip at the expected time
- `disc_fraction` result is unchanged when there is no eclipse
