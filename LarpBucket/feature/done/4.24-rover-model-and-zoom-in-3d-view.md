# 4.24 Rover model at the site, and a zoom to it, in the 3D view

Status: done
Owner: Aiken
Source: follows 4.9 (3D terrain view), which is not in scope.md yet. Requested by the 3D view's owner.
Note: done. New Unity build in `frontend/public/unity/` with a rover at the site; a **Zoom to rover** button in `frontend/src/components/TerrainView3D.tsx`, so it shows in both UIs.

## Description
Stand a rover at the site in the 3D view, at its real size, and keep the red pin. Because a 3 m rover is only a few pixels until the camera is within about 100 m, add a button that takes the orbit camera to it and back.

## Expected user interaction
In Orbit view, **Zoom to rover** glides the camera to 14 m from the site, low and side-on: the rover with its shadow, the pin floating just above it. **Zoom out** glides back to the overview. Dragging or scrolling during the glide stops it. The rover does not move.

## Done when
- A rover stands at the site at true size, with the pin above it
- The button reaches it and returns, without a cut
- The rover is not drawn from far away or in surface view

## Solution
**Chosen:** The model is NASA's Mars 2020 Perseverance from NASA 3D Resources ("free and without copyright"), thinned from about 200,000 to 82,000 triangles with 256 px textures. It stands at the site, tilted to the slope, and casts and receives shadows. It is drawn only within 3 km of the camera and hidden in surface view, where the camera stands in its place. The page sends `SendMessage("Bridge", "SetOrbitDistance", metres)`; the camera glides there over 1.6 s in equal steps of scale. Scroll zoom now goes in to 8 m (it stopped at 200 m).

**Strengths**
- A NASA model, with no licence question.
- Costs nothing when it cannot be seen: frame rate on integrated graphics is unchanged at the usual distance.
- One button, since scrolling from the overview to the rover would take over a hundred notches.

**Limitations**
- It is a Mars rover standing in for a lunar one. NASA has not published a model of VIPER, whose power figures the analysis uses (6.7).
- Adds 1.9 MB to the 3D view's first download (about 25 MB to 27 MB).
- The rover faces a fixed way and does not drive.
- Its brightness is set by eye: the scene's Sun is far brighter than life so that grazing-lit ground shows, so the rover is dimmed to match, with a faint glow standing in for light bounced off the ground.

**Alternatives considered**
| Option | Why not chosen | Trade-off vs chosen |
|---|---|---|
| A low-poly public-domain rover | The owner asked for a NASA model | A tenth of the download |
| A paid VIPER model | About $50 to $70, one marked editorial use only | The right rover |
| Draw the rover oversized so it shows from afar | The view is true scale everywhere else | No button needed |
| Replace the pin with the rover | The pin is what marks the site from a distance | One marker fewer |
