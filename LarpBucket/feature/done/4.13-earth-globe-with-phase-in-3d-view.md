# 4.13 Draw the Earth as a globe with its phase in the 3D view

Status: done
Owner: Aiken
Source: follows 4.9 (3D terrain view), which is not in scope.md yet. Requested by the 3D view's owner.
Note: done. The Earth in orbit and surface views is NASA's Blue Marble on a sphere, lit from the Sun's side. It is part of the new build in `frontend/public/unity/`.

## Description
The Earth in the 3D view was a flat blue circle. Draw it as the Earth: a photo map on a sphere at its real angular size (1.9°), lit from the direction of the Sun so it shows the phase it would have from the Moon.

## Expected user interaction
In Orbit view or Surface view the Earth looks like the Earth. As the scrubber moves, its lit part changes with the angle between the Sun and the Earth: full when they are on opposite sides of the sky, a crescent when they are close, dark when the Earth covers the Sun.

## Done when
- The Earth shows land, ocean and ice, at 1.9° across
- Its lit side faces the Sun at every scrubber step
- No change to the Sun and Earth angles the view is given

## Solution
**Chosen:** In the Unity build, the Earth is a sphere with NASA Visible Earth's "Blue Marble: Land Surface, Ocean Color and Sea Ice" map (public domain; NASA Goddard Space Flight Center, image by Reto Stöckli, enhancements by Robert Simmon), reduced to 1024 x 512. A small shader lights it from the Sun direction the page sends, with a soft day-night line and a thin blue edge for the atmosphere. Nothing changes in the React code: the view still only receives `sunAz`, `sunEl`, `earthAz`, `earthEl`.

**Strengths**
- The phase is not extra data. It follows from the two directions the view already has, so it cannot disagree with the analysis.
- The Earth has its own lighting, so the very bright scene Sun (set so grazing-lit ground stays visible) and the eclipse dimming don't wash it out or darken it.
- NASA imagery, about 120 kB.

**Limitations**
- One fixed side of the Earth faces the Moon. The real Earth turns once a day, and time of day is not part of what the view is sent.
- The Earth is drawn with its north pole pointing down, which is right near the Moon's south pole. Away from the pole it would be tilted differently, but the 3D view has no terrain there.
- The night side is drawn faintly (about 5% brightness) so the Earth can still be found when it is a thin crescent. The real night side would be black.
- No clouds.

**Alternatives considered**
| Option | Why not chosen | Trade-off vs chosen |
|---|---|---|
| A photo on a flat disc, always fully lit | A full Earth next to the Sun would be wrong, and it was the look to get away from | Simplest |
| Light the sphere with the scene's Sun light | That light is about ten times too bright for it and dims in an eclipse | No shader of our own |
| Spin the Earth from the time stamp | The view is not sent the time, and the sub-lunar longitude would need its own calculation | The correct continents would face the Moon |
