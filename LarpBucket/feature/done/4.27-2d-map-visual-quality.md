# 4.27 2D map: sharper, unstretched and nicer to look at

Status: done
Owner: Tony Pham
Source: follow-up to 4.11 (2D bird's-eye view, `doc/2d-view.md`). Supports scope.md > Core 3 Horizon panorama and Core 5 (time scrubber).
Depends on: 4.11, BUG-009
Note: done. Canvas follows its real box (no stretch) and opens filling it. Map painted at 2x with bilinear interpolation and a smoothstep shadow edge. Relief lit from the Sun plus north-west, with ambient occlusion and warm/cool colours. Labels have a halo and stay on the map; rays glow. Optional 50 m grid not done.

## Description
The 2D bird's-eye map (`TerrainMap.tsx`, `terrain2d.ts`) looks grainy, stretched and flat, especially in the board UI's detail view. Improve how it looks without changing what it shows: the site, the Sun and Earth directions from `POST /analyze`, and the ground in shadow at the scrubber's time.

In order of payoff for effort:
1. **Fix the stretch** (BUG-009): size the canvas to its real box, not a fixed 16:9.
2. **Less grainy**:
   - Keep image smoothing on at every zoom, not only below zoom 4.
   - Soften the shadow edge. Blur the `lit` mask slightly (for example a 3×3 average) before painting.
3. **Nicer look** (`terrain2d.ts`):
   - Light the relief from the Sun's real azimuth, or blend that with the current fixed north-west light, so relief and shadows agree.
   - Use a warm tint for sunlit ground and a deep cool tint for shadow.
   - Optionally add light ambient occlusion so crater floors and slope feet read as depth.
   - Give label text a dark halo so it stays readable on bright ground, and keep labels from being clipped at the edge.
   - Fill the box rather than leaving black bars at the sides (open zoomed to cover the box, or similar).
4. **Optional, if time allows**: serve `GET /terrain` at 50 m cells instead of 100 m, sent as compact int16 binary or base64 rather than a JSON number list, so the download stays well under the 4.11 target. Keep the shadow pass fast enough to follow the scrubber (a Web Worker or a coarser shadow grid if needed).

Keep the stack as it is: plain `<canvas>`, no new libraries. Moving to WebGL shading is out of scope for this ticket.

## Expected user interaction
The user opens bird's-eye view in either UI. The map fills its panel without stretching: the site marker is a circle and craters keep their true shape. The terrain looks smooth when zoomed in, with no blocky cells and no staircase shadow edges. Relief shading matches where the Sun is. Sun and Earth labels are readable and are not cut off. Dragging the scrubber still moves the shadows and arrows smoothly.

## Done when
- `npm run build` passes
- The site marker is round and the map is not stretched at any panel size, in the board and classic UIs (BUG-009 closed)
- No visible blocky cells or stepped shadow edges at max zoom
- Relief lighting follows the current Sun azimuth
- Sun and Earth labels are readable on bright and dark ground and are not clipped
- Scrubbing stays smooth (shadow update under ~30 ms on a laptop)
- If item 4 is done: backend tests pass and the `/terrain` response stays well under 5 MB

## Solution
**Chosen:** Changes in `frontend/src/terrain2d.ts` (shading) and `frontend/src/components/TerrainMap.tsx` (canvas). `doc/2d-view.md` is updated to match.
- Stretch (BUG-009): the ResizeObserver tracks width and height, and the bitmap matches the box. `startView()` opens at "cover" zoom (`max(w,h)/min(w,h)`), so there are no black side bars; zooming out to 1 still shows the whole square.
- Grain: `paint()` writes a 2x (`UPSAMPLE`) image. Brightness and sunlight are bilinearly interpolated between cells, and sunlight goes through `smoothstep(0.25, 0.75)`, so shadow edges are crisp without 100 m steps. Canvas smoothing stays on at every zoom.
- Look:
  - `relief(grid, sunAz)` lights 65% from the Sun and 35% from the north-west, 30° up, with contrast stretched about flat ground. Normals are cached per grid in a `WeakMap`, so re-lighting costs about 1 ms.
  - New `occlusion()`: height relative to a 600 m box mean (summed-area table), which darkens hollows.
  - Colour ramps: warm sunlit, deep blue shadow that keeps relief visible, neutral grey before Analyze.
  - On the canvas: a vignette, glowing Sun/Earth rays with a dark underlay, and halo text. Labels are measured and clamped inside the canvas.

**Strengths**
- No new libraries and no backend change; `/terrain` and `sunlight()` are untouched, so `HeroMap.tsx` is unaffected.
- Relief and cast shadows now come from the same direction, and the north-west share keeps craters from looking like domes when the Sun is in the south.
- Measured in Node on the real Shackleton and Apollo 11 grids: about 27 ms per scrubber step with the Sun up (shadows ~13.5, relief ~0.7, paint ~12), within the ~30 ms target. Before vs after renders were compared side by side.

**Limitations**
- The ~30 ms target holds with the Sun well up. Near the horizon, the existing `sunlight()` pass takes ~80 ms on its own; that predates this ticket and is filed as BUG-010.
- Still 100 m data; 2x interpolation makes it smooth, not more detailed. Away from the pole (474 m source) the map is soft.
- Interpolation can round off very small lit or shadowed specks (one cell).
- Not checked in a real browser by the agent (no headless browser here): the renders came from the same code in Node. The canvas-side changes (aspect, vignette, labels) need a visual check in the app.

**Alternatives considered**
| Option | Why not chosen | Trade-off vs chosen |
|---|---|---|
| 50 m `/terrain` grid in int16 (optional item 4) | 4x cells and ~2x ray steps would make `sunlight()` ~8x slower, far over the scrub budget, without a worker or a faster algorithm | Real detail at the pole vs smooth-but-100 m |
| WebGL shading in a fragment shader | The ticket keeps the stack to plain canvas | Per-pixel smoothness at any zoom and free scrubbing |
| Let canvas smoothing upscale a 1x image | Bilinear on a 0/1 shadow mask gives a 100 m blurry ramp, not an edge | ~10 ms cheaper per step |
| 3x upsampling | Paint ~30 ms, total ~48 ms per step | Slightly crisper edges at deep zoom |
| Relief lit only from the Sun | South-lit relief makes craters look like domes (relief inversion) | Fully physical, but harder to read |
